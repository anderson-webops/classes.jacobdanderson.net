## GEOA1 Objects, Notation and Angle Relationships

1. Ray AB begins at (0,0) and extends rightward through B; ray BA begins at (2,0) and extends leftward through A. They are different sets, although the containing line is the same.
2. They are acute, right, obtuse and straight. The complement of 35 degrees is 55 degrees, and its supplement is 145 degrees.
3. The vertical angle is 68 degrees. Each adjacent angle forms a linear pair with it, so each is 180-68=112 degrees.
4. AB as a length is 2 coordinate units; segment AB is a geometric set of points including both endpoints. B lies on segment AC. All four coordinates lie in the same xy-plane, so they are coplanar.

## GEOA2 Conditionals, Counterexamples and Deduction

1. The converse says every isosceles triangle is equilateral. It is false: side lengths 3,3,4 form an isosceles triangle with unequal third side. The original implication is true under the stated inclusive definition.
2. At n=1 the expression is 2, a counterexample to oddness. For every integer, n^2+n=n(n+1) is even because one of two consecutive integers is even.
3. The conditional truth values in that order are true, false, true, true. A false hypothesis does not produce a counterexample to the implication.
4. One right angle is insufficient. A rectangle requires four right angles; the remaining angles need evidence rather than an inference from one observed angle.

## GEOA3 Proofs and First Invalid Steps

1. The first invalid transition is 3x=15 to x=15; division by 3 gives x=5. The original lengths are 13 and 13, whose sum is 26.
2. Midpoint gives AM=MB, so AB=2AM. Thus 12x-4=2(4x+3), giving 4x=10 and x=5/2. Both pieces measure 13 and AB=26.
3. The bisector makes angles ABD and DBC congruent. Angle addition makes their sum 84 degrees, so each measures 42 degrees.
4. The transitive property of equality gives equal lengths. Equality of lengths does not establish collinearity; their positions require separate information.

## GEOA4 Parallel Lines and Transversals

1. Vertical angles are congruent at each intersection, independently of the parallel-line theorem; each vertical partner is 70 degrees.
2. Their sum is 180 degrees: 5x+55=180, so x=25. The measures are 65 and 115 degrees.
3. The corresponding-angle converse establishes that the two lines are parallel. Equal vertical angles at one intersection alone do not establish parallelism with another line.

## GEOA5 Coordinate Distance, Midpoint and Slope

2. The first pair has equal slopes and different intercepts, so the lines are parallel. The second pair has slope product -1, so it is perpendicular. Equal slopes alone also allow two descriptions of the same line.
3. The horizontal line is y=-1 with slope 0; the vertical line is x=3 with undefined slope. They are perpendicular.
