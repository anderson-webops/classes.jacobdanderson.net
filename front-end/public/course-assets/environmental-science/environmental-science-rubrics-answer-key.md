# Intro to Environmental Science Rubrics and Answer Key

## Common Evidence and Decision Rubric

Assess each category as emerging, developing, secure, or transferable:

- **Question and boundary:** identifies place, time, system, and comparison.
- **Evidence:** cites relevant data with units, baseline, date, scale, and source.
- **Mechanism:** explains matter, energy, organism, or human-system connections.
- **Inference:** distinguishes observation, correlation, attribution, projection, and preference.
- **Uncertainty:** names missing data, confounders, model limits, and plausible alternatives.
- **Distribution:** identifies who or what benefits, bears burden, or lacks representation.
- **Decision:** compares alternatives using visible criteria and tradeoffs.
- **Monitoring:** defines indicators, timing, ownership, and adaptation triggers.
- **Accessibility and integrity:** uses text equivalents, protects location and personal data, and preserves citations.

## Ecosystem and Succession Key

A biome is a broad climate- and community-associated region; an ecosystem is a bounded interaction model. Organism presence alone does not define either. Disturbance is an event or pressure; succession is a time-dependent pattern of community change. Recovery need not return a system to an identical prior state.

Strong explanations distinguish resistance, recovery, reorganization, and regime shift; compare a baseline; and avoid calling every change degradation or every fire unnatural.

## Food Web and Biodiversity Key

Arrows follow the stated convention consistently. Energy flows through producers, consumers, and decomposers and disperses as heat; nutrients cycle among living and nonliving reservoirs. Decomposers do not recycle energy.

In the population table, predator observations per survey hour are 0.45, 0.425, approximately 0.458, and 0.30. The year-three raw decline is partly explained by lower effort. Carrying capacity is a dynamic model shaped by resources, interactions, disturbance, and time rather than a fixed species number.

## Food System Comparison Key

No fictional meal is universally best. Valid comparisons:

1. state a functional unit;
2. compare the same indicator and boundary;
3. identify production, processing, transport, storage, and waste assumptions;
4. preserve nutrition, culture, affordability, access, and missing-data boundaries;
5. avoid using a personal food journal or making a recommendation for an individual.

## Weather Climate and Attribution Key

Weather concerns short-term conditions; climate concerns distributions and patterns across longer periods. A single cold or hot event cannot establish or refute a climate trend. A trend does not by itself identify its cause.

The instructional station series supports a warming-pattern claim within the supplied baseline, but station movement and one incomplete period require review. Attribution relies on multiple lines of evidence and physical mechanisms. A policy preference requires additional feasibility, distribution, and values analysis.

## Water and Ocean Systems Key

Watersheds are defined by drainage to an outlet. Downstream water condition can reflect land cover, runoff, groundwater, infrastructure, discharge, season, and sampling design. Central has the highest impervious cover and storm turbidity; South has the highest nitrate index. Neither observation alone proves one source.

Ocean acidification is a change in carbonate chemistry associated with carbon dioxide uptake. A falling pH is more acidic even when pH remains above 7. Coastal variability and other stressors remain relevant.

## Energy Lifecycle Key

Every energy option has upstream, operational, infrastructure, land, material, reliability, and end-of-life considerations. Electricity is an energy carrier rather than inherently renewable or nonrenewable. Compare alternatives for one location and functional unit; identify storage, transmission, and balancing assumptions.

A recommendation is conditional when evidence is qualitative or incomplete. “Renewable,” “low-carbon,” and “clean” do not mean impact-free.

## Pollution and Distribution Key

Release, concentration, exposure, dose, risk, and observed health outcome are distinct. Zone A has the highest reported-release index, while Zone B has the highest modeled-exposure indicator and largest represented population. That difference demonstrates why facility releases cannot substitute for an exposure analysis.

Distributional review compares environmental burdens, benefits, representation, uncertainty, and cumulative conditions without inferring individual health or collecting private demographic data.

## Scenario Modeling Key

Deep-time, historical, present, trend, projection, and preference claims require different evidence. A future scenario is conditional on its assumptions; it is not a forecast stated with certainty. Compare scenarios against the same baseline and show which differences arise from physical drivers, decisions, or values.

## Capstone Decision and Monitoring Rubric

The capstone defense answers:

1. What is the baseline and narrowly defined problem?
2. Which alternatives were compared, including no action?
3. Which evidence supports each criterion, and what is missing?
4. Which causal mechanism is proposed?
5. Who benefits, who bears burden, and who was not represented?
6. What tradeoff or rebound effect could weaken the proposal?
7. What leading, outcome, and adverse-effect indicators will be monitored?
8. Who owns implementation, when is review, and what triggers adaptation or rollback?
9. What evidence would change the recommendation?
10. How did feedback revise the design or decision rule?

A polished pitch cannot replace the evidence packet. If a criterion lacks evidence, narrow the claim, gather data, or present the decision as conditional.
