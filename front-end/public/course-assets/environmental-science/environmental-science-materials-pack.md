# Intro to Environmental Science Materials Pack

These supplied datasets and cases keep every investigation possible without outdoor travel, household purchasing, food disclosure, political advocacy, or personal location data. Unless an authoritative source is named, numerical values are fictional instructional data for comparing methods rather than observations about a real community.

## Course Evidence and Data Routine

For each investigation, record:

1. the question, place, time span, and system boundary;
2. the datum, map layer, observation, or source statement used;
3. units, baseline, comparison, and uncertainty;
4. the mechanism or system model that connects evidence to the claim;
5. one alternative explanation or confounding factor;
6. who or what may benefit, bear cost, or remain unrepresented;
7. a changed-condition prediction and one monitoring indicator;
8. a citation, text equivalent for visuals, and privacy note.

Correlation, temporal order, mechanism, attribution, and policy preference are different claims. A map shows where variables overlap; it does not prove that one variable caused another.

## Food Web and Biodiversity Cases

### Grassland web

Producers: native grasses and flowering plants. Primary consumers: grasshoppers, rabbits, and seed-eating rodents. Higher consumers: snakes, foxes, and hawks. Decomposers: fungi, bacteria, and detritivores.

Disturbance options:

- producer index falls from 100 to 65 after drought;
- a nonnative plant occupies 30% of sampled quadrats;
- predator observations fall while sampling effort also falls;
- fire returns after a long period of suppression.

The cases support hypotheses, not automatic conclusions. Observation count is affected by abundance, detectability, area, season, and effort.

| ------ | -------------------------------------------------------: | ------------: | -----------------------: | ------------------ |
| A      |                                                  -0.2 °C |             8 |                        4 | 30 complete years  |

| C      |                                                  +0.5 °C |            15 |                        7 | 29 complete years  |
| D      |                                                  +0.8 °C |            19 |                        8 | station moved once |

One snowy day is weather. A long, quality-controlled series can support a climate trend. Attribution requires physical understanding and comparison of multiple causes, not only trend detection.

### Claim ladder

1. The local event occurred.
2. The event's frequency or intensity differs from a historical baseline.
3. A broader climate trend changes the event's likelihood or severity.
4. A specified forcing contributes to the broader trend.
5. A particular response option is preferable.

Each rung needs different evidence. Accepting one does not automatically prove the next.

## Soil Ocean and Carbon System Table

| Reservoir or process | Carbon or material role          | Time scale                     | Environmental connection          | Common model error                         |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| vegetation           | carbon uptake and biomass        | seasons to decades             | food webs and habitat             | treating storage as permanent              |
| soil                 | organic matter, water, nutrients | years to centuries             | fertility, erosion, decomposition | treating soil as inert dirt                |
| atmosphere           | greenhouse gases and transport   | days to centuries              | weather and climate forcing       | confusing concentration with emission rate |
| surface ocean        | heat and carbon exchange         | days to centuries              | marine chemistry and circulation  | saying lower pH means acidic below pH 7    |
| sediment and rock    | long-term storage and weathering | thousands to millions of years | geology and nutrient cycles       | collapsing deep time into human time       |

Ocean acidification means a decline in pH caused by changing carbonate chemistry; ocean water can become more acidic while remaining above pH 7. Local coastal chemistry can also vary with runoff, respiration, mixing, and season.

Build at least two plausible future scenarios from the same baseline. Identify which differences come from physical assumptions, human decisions, and values.
