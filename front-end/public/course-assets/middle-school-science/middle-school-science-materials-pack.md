# Middle School Integrated Science Materials Pack

These supplied phenomena, structured descriptions, and fictional instructional datasets make every core and stretch route possible with a shared screen and paper or digital notes. No task requires chemicals, organisms, electrical construction, outdoor sampling, household experiments, personal health information, family traits, ancestry, location disclosure, a camera, or specialized equipment.

## Common Science Notebook and Source Record

Use these fields for every investigation:

1. phenomenon, question, and source title;
2. system boundary, scale, and time span;
3. direct observations or raw values with units;
4. variable table, graph, map annotation, particle model, or system diagram;
5. claim;
6. two or more traceable evidence points;
7. reasoning or mechanism;
8. alternate explanation, model limitation, or uncertainty;
9. changed-condition prediction;
10. revision after comparison, feedback, or test evidence.

Speaking, selecting, dictating, drawing, typing, and handwriting can demonstrate the same science understanding. Visual work includes a text description, audio has a transcript or event table, and color is paired with labels, symbols, or patterns.

## Reasoning Models and Data Cases

### Pond survey

The values are fictional. The three teams observed the same marked shoreline area during the same week, but they spent different amounts of time there.

| Team | Observation time | Minnow observations | Heron observations | Water temperature | Notes missing |
| ---- | ---------------: | ------------------: | -----------------: | ----------------: | ------------- |
| A    |          2 hours |                  18 |                  2 |           18.2 °C | none          |
| B    |          5 hours |                  35 |                  5 |           18.5 °C | one pH value  |
| C    |           1 hour |                  11 |                  0 |           19.0 °C | cloud cover   |

Raw counts and observations per hour answer different questions. Observation does not establish the true population size, and temperature differences do not by themselves explain animal counts.

### Flawed investigation

A student wants to know whether shaded pond water supports more minnows. The student observes one shaded site for five hours on Monday morning and one sunny site for one hour on Friday afternoon. The sites differ in depth, vegetation, water movement, and observer. The student reports raw counts without temperature or observation effort and concludes that shade caused the difference.

Revision fields:

- measurable question;
- changed condition;
- measured result;
- comparable sites or repeated observations;
- constants and recorded context;
- rate or sampling measure;
- exclusion and missing-data rule;
- claim type the design can support.

### Representation cards

- **Bar graph:** compare minnow observation rates among teams.
- **Line graph:** display a value measured repeatedly through ordered time.
- **Scatterplot:** compare paired quantitative values such as temperature and observation rate across many comparable surveys.
- **Table:** preserve exact values, missing entries, and observation conditions.

One deliberate error for critique: a draft graph labels the vertical axis “Minnow population” even though the table contains observations, not a population census.

## Ecosystems and Human Impact Cases

### Fictional estuary web

The arrow convention is food or energy source → receiving organism.

- sunlight → algae and marsh grass;
- algae → zooplankton → small fish → heron;
- marsh grass detritus → worms → shorebird;
- small fish → larger fish → seal;
- dead material and waste → decomposers;
- decomposer activity moves matter toward water and sediment reservoirs.

Energy disperses as heat through transfers. Atoms and nutrients continue through organisms, waste, decomposers, water, air, and sediment.

### Unequal survey effort

| Site    | Survey area | Survey time | Species observed | Individuals observed | Dissolved oxygen |
| ------- | ----------: | ----------: | ---------------: | -------------------: | ---------------: |
| North   |      100 m² |     2 hours |                6 |                   48 |         7.8 mg/L |
| Central |      100 m² |     4 hours |                8 |                   76 |         6.2 mg/L |
| South   |       50 m² |     4 hours |                5 |                   45 |         4.7 mg/L |

Species richness is the number of species observed. Relative abundance describes proportions among species. Evenness describes how similar those proportions are. These values require comparable sampling before site-level conclusions.

### Runoff pathway case

| Station          | Nitrate before | Nitrate after | Algae index before | Algae index after | Fish count before | Fish count after |
| ---------------- | -------------: | ------------: | -----------------: | ----------------: | ----------------: | ---------------: |
| upstream control |       1.1 mg/L |      1.2 mg/L |                 18 |                19 |                22 |               21 |
| channel mouth    |       1.3 mg/L |      4.8 mg/L |                 20 |                46 |                19 |               12 |
| downstream       |       1.2 mg/L |      3.2 mg/L |                 21 |                38 |                20 |               15 |

The table supports a release-and-ecological-response investigation. It does not provide human exposure or health-outcome data.

### Decision options

| Option                  | Expected ecological benefit | Relative cost | Time to begin | Main uncertainty                     |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------- | ------------: | ------------: | ------------------------------------ |
| vegetated buffer        | medium to high              |             3 |      3 months | maintenance quality                  |
| stormwater storage      | high                        |             5 |     12 months | construction disruption              |

Use 1 for low and 5 for high relative cost. A recommendation includes a monitoring indicator, threshold, and revision trigger.

### Climate claim ladder cards

1. **Detection:** Has a variable changed beyond short-term variation in the supplied record?
2. **Attribution:** Which drivers best explain the detected change when several evidence lines are compared?
3. **Impact:** Which observed or projected system responses are connected to the change?
4. **Decision:** Which response meets stated criteria under uncertainty?

### Energy-account cards

1. battery-powered lamp: chemical store → electrical transfer → light output plus thermal transfer to surroundings;
2. rolling cart slowing: kinetic store decreases while thermal stores of cart, surface, and surroundings increase;
3. phone charging: electrical transfer changes chemical store and thermal surroundings;
4. falling object: gravitational interaction store changes while kinetic store increases before impact.

### Thermal model cards

- conduction: energy transfer through interactions within or between materials;
- convection: energy transfer associated with bulk fluid motion;
- radiation: energy transfer by electromagnetic radiation, including through empty space;
- temperature: related to average particle kinetic energy in the course model;
- thermal energy: depends on particle energy, amount, and material.

“Cold flowed into the container” is the intentional misconception to revise.

## Waves Circuits and Signals Cases

### Wave representation table

| Signal | Amplitude | Frequency | Representation | Encoded bit            |
| ------ | --------: | --------: | -------------- | ---------------------- |
| P1     |   2 units |      4 Hz | time trace     | 0                      |
| P2     |   5 units |      4 Hz | time trace     | 1                      |
| P3     |   2 units |      8 Hz | time trace     | 0 under amplitude code |
| P4     |   5 units |      8 Hz | time trace     | 1 under amplitude code |

In this fictional amplitude code, low amplitude represents 0 and high amplitude represents 1. Frequency varies independently and could be used for a different code.

### Pulse message

Message packets use four data bits followed by one parity bit. The parity bit makes the total number of 1s even.

| Packet | Received bits | Status                     |
| ------ | ------------- | -------------------------- |
| A      | 1 0 1 0 0     | even parity                |
| B      | 1 1 0 1 1     | even parity                |
| C      | 1 0 0 0 0     | odd parity; possible error |

The parity check detects some errors but cannot correct every error or identify every multi-bit error.

### Circuit cases

The source is the same in all simplified circuit states.

| Circuit | Arrangement                             | Both lamps connected                       | One lamp removed                |
| ------- | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------- |
| S       | two lamps in one series path            | both glow dimly in the supplied state      | path opens; neither glows       |
| P       | two lamps on separate parallel branches | both glow at supplied reference brightness | remaining branch stays complete |

Current is not used up by the first lamp. Brightness is qualitative unless a measurement is supplied.

### Field and device cards

- bar magnet: field model has direction convention outside the magnet from north toward south;
- electromagnet: current in a coil produces a magnetic interaction that changes with current, turns, core, and geometry;
- motor model: electrical transfer contributes to motion plus thermal and sound transfers;
- generator model: mechanical input contributes to electrical transfer plus losses.

Audio signals have waveform and bit-table alternatives. Visual signals use text, shape, and pattern rather than color alone.

## Space Systems and Capstone Cases

### Earth-moon-sun model cards

- day and night: Earth rotation changes whether a location faces the Sun;
- Moon phases: half the Moon is illuminated by the Sun, while the visible fraction changes with geometry;
- eclipse: a particular alignment places one body in another's shadow;
- seasons: Earth’s axial tilt changes Sun angle and day length through the orbit;
- orbit: tangential motion and inward gravitational acceleration produce a curved path.

Moon phases are not caused by Earth's shadow, and seasons are not caused mainly by changing Earth-Sun distance.
