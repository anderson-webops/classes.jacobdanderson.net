# Middle School Integrated Science Rubrics and Answer Key

## Common Evidence and Model Rubric

Assess each category as starting, developing, secure, or extending:

- **Question and boundary:** defines the phenomenon, system, scale, time span, variable, or design problem.
- **Source and evidence:** identifies the source and cites exact observations, values, units, map features, or model states.
- **Representation:** uses a readable table, graph, map, particle model, field model, diagram, or comparison matrix with labels and text equivalents.
- **Reasoning:** connects evidence to a mechanism rather than repeating the claim or values.
- **Claim strength:** distinguishes description, pattern, correlation, causal explanation, prediction, and recommendation.
- **Alternative and uncertainty:** compares another explanation, identifies missing evidence, or states a model limitation.
- **Revision:** preserves the first version and explains a substantive evidence-based change.
- **Accessibility and safety:** avoids required physical materials or personal disclosure and provides non-color, non-audio, and noninteractive routes.

Secure core work completes the bounded claim, representation, exact evidence, reasoning, and limitation. Extending work compares an alternative, quantifies a relationship or uncertainty when data permit, tests a changed condition, and revises from evidence.

## Reasoning Models and Data Key

Pond observation rates are:

- Team A: 9 minnow observations/hour and 1 heron observation/hour.
- Team B: 7 minnow observations/hour and 1 heron observation/hour.
- Team C: 11 minnow observations/hour and 0 heron observations/hour.

The rates do not estimate true population size without a sampling model. Team C has the highest minnow observation rate but the shortest observation time. The 0 heron count is not proof that no herons were present.

The flawed shade investigation confounds shade with day, time, observation length, depth, vegetation, water movement, and observer. A stronger design uses comparable sites or repeated observations across conditions, equal effort, recorded context, a defined response measure, and a claim no stronger than the design.

The draft vertical-axis label must say minnow observations or minnow observations per hour, not population. Bar graphs fit team comparisons; line graphs require ordered repeated measurements; scatterplots require many paired quantitative observations.

## Genetics Variation and Selection Key

Genotype records selected inherited variants. Phenotype is an observed or measured outcome shaped by genotype, environment, development, and chance. One gene does not guarantee one complex outcome, and classroom probability does not predict an individual with certainty.

Dark-shell frequency changes from 0.28 to 0.73 over four generations. The supplied detection evidence and inherited shell-shade model support natural selection as a plausible explanation. The packet omits migration, mate choice, disease, and predator change, so repeated populations or added evidence strengthen the conclusion.

Small samples S1 and S2 deviate more visibly from 50% than large samples L1 and L2. This illustrates sampling variation, not a rule that every large sample must be exactly 50%.

Valid explanations describe population frequencies across generations. Organisms do not choose or need a mutation because the environment demands it. No personal or family-trait data belongs in the assessment.

## Ecosystems and Human Impact Key

Food-web arrows point source → receiver. Energy enters through producers in this model and disperses as heat; matter continues through organisms, waste, decomposers, water, air, and sediment.

The survey table is not directly comparable because site area and effort differ. North reports 24 individuals/hour across 100 m², Central 19/hour across 100 m², and South 11.25/hour across 50 m². These rough rates still do not correct every detectability or habitat difference.

The runoff table supports a pathway from nutrient-rich release toward higher nitrate and algae indices plus lower fish counts near and downstream from the channel. It does not establish human exposure, individual health outcomes, or one exclusive ecological mechanism.

A decision can favor different options under different criteria. A complete recommendation names the weights, burden and benefit distribution, indicator, baseline, threshold, and response. Monitoring alone gathers information but does not directly reduce the release.

## Earth Weather Climate and Geologic Key

The seven-day sequence shows falling pressure and rising humidity before the largest precipitation on day 4, then rising pressure and drying conditions. This supports a bounded forecast discussion, not a climate conclusion.

The fictional temperature anomaly increases across the five decadal periods. That is detection evidence for the supplied region. Attribution requires independent evidence about drivers, mechanisms, competing explanations, and model agreement. A single storm cannot confirm or refute a long-term climate trend.

Plate-tectonic support becomes stronger when independent evidence converges:

- youngest seafloor and symmetric magnetic patterns support spreading at a ridge;
- deep earthquakes, a trench, and a volcanic arc support a subduction model;
- measured directions support current relative motion;
- fossils and rock units support past connection when combined with other evidence.

Coastline resemblance alone is insufficient. Hazard describes a potential event; risk includes exposure and vulnerability. A classroom map compresses time, distance, depth, and measurement uncertainty.

## Matter Atoms and Reaction Key

For A₂ + B₂ → 2AB, one A₂ particle and one B₂ particle contain two A atoms and two B atoms, matching the two AB product particles. Coefficients change particle counts; changing subscripts would change represented identity.

The sealed table conserves 12 A atoms, 12 B atoms, and 125.4 g within 0.1 g resolution. In the open setup, gas can leave the measured container, so lower measured mass does not mean destroyed matter.

Case classifications:

- melting/refreezing and dissolving/recovery: physical changes in the supplied model;
- persistent solid plus new property evidence: chemical change supported;
- warming alone: insufficient evidence;
- dilution color change: physical explanation available;
- A₂ and B₂ rearranging to AB: chemical change supported in the model.

Gas, color, temperature, precipitate, odor, conductivity, pH, or light can contribute evidence but no single clue settles every case.

## Energy Heat and Engineering Key

After 30 minutes, Design B retains the highest water temperature at 55 °C, followed by A at 49 °C and C at 45 °C. B also has the highest cost and mass, while C has the lowest cost and highest recyclable-material score. A recommendation depends on weights and constraints.

Energy accounts include the selected system plus transfers to surroundings. Reduced useful output does not mean energy disappeared. A low-efficiency model tracks more energy into thermal or other non-target stores.

Heating is energy transfer caused by a temperature difference. Temperature and total thermal energy are not interchangeable. Conduction, convection, and radiation can occur together; “cold flows” is replaced with energy transferring from the warmer system toward the cooler surroundings or object.

## Forces Motion and Graphs Key

Cart one-second position changes are +2 m, +4 m, +6 m, +6 m, +4 m, 0 m, -4 m, and -6 m. The draft claim of constant speed from 0–3 seconds is false because the magnitude of position change grows across those intervals.

From 0–8 seconds, total distance is 32 m and displacement is +12 m. Average speed is 4 m/s; average velocity is +1.5 m/s east. These averages hide changes within the trip.

Force results:

- book: 0 N, consistent with no acceleration;
- speeding cart: 5 N east, consistent with eastward acceleration;
- steadily moving puck: 0 N, consistent with constant velocity;
- east-moving slowing sled: 4 N west, acceleration opposite its eastward velocity.

Balanced force does not require rest. Motion does not require net force. Third-law pairs act on different objects and therefore do not cancel on one free-body diagram.

## Waves Circuits and Signals Key

P1 and P3 encode 0 under the supplied amplitude rule; P2 and P4 encode 1. P1/P2 differ in amplitude at the same frequency, while P1/P3 differ in frequency at the same amplitude. Amplitude and frequency are separate properties.

Packets A and B contain an even total number of 1s. Packet C has odd parity and flags a possible error. One parity bit cannot correct the bit or detect every possible multi-bit error.

In the supplied series circuit, removing one lamp opens the only path. In the parallel circuit, the other branch remains complete. Current is not used up by the first component, and brightness alone is not a precise current or power measurement.

Field lines are a representation of direction and relative pattern, not physical threads. Motor and generator models trace energy transfer plus thermal and sound losses. A robust communication design includes error checking and a text, shape, pattern, or visual route that does not require sound or color perception.

## Space Systems and Capstone Key

Earth rotation explains day and night. Moon phases arise from changing Sun-moon-Earth geometry and the visible fraction of the Moon's illuminated half; eclipses require particular shadow alignments. Seasons arise mainly from axial tilt changing Sun angle and day length, not Earth-Sun distance.

Orbiting objects remain under gravitational acceleration. Tangential motion plus inward acceleration produces a curved path. “No gravity in space” is incorrect.

The rounded planet table supports consistent-unit comparisons. Jupiter's diameter is about 10.97 times Earth's using the supplied values, while its mean distance is 5.2 AU. An ordinary page cannot preserve both diameter and distance scale.

Spectral lines, shifts, brightness changes, and color distributions support inferences only with reference data, instrument context, and model assumptions. Capstone work labels observed, calculated, inferred, modeled, and predicted features separately.

## Capstone Defense Rubric

The final defense answers:

1. What phenomenon, question, or design problem is being studied?
2. What is the system boundary, scale, and time frame?
3. Where did every evidence item come from?
4. Which observations or values directly support the claim?
5. What does the graph, map, model, or design comparison represent?
6. What mechanism connects evidence and claim?
7. Which alternate explanation or design was considered?
8. What uncertainty, omission, or failed criterion matters most?
9. What changed between the first and revised version, and why?
10. What changed condition or monitoring result would alter the conclusion?
11. Can the work be understood without color, audio, physical materials, or personal disclosure?

A polished presentation cannot replace traceable evidence, model honesty, alternatives, and substantive revision.
