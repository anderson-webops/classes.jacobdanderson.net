# Picture book practice responses

## opinion seed practice

Tastiest is an opinion. Four mangoes and a seed inside are checkable claims. A library opinion could be supported by a picture of many book topics and a separate picture of a quiet reading corner. Different opinions and pictures are valid when the connection is explained.

## audience seed challenge

A concrete reason could show space to practice floating. A beginning swimmer might need a shallow practice area in the picture; an experienced swimmer might value marked lanes. Audience changes the relevant detail without turning the preference into a fact.

## parts of speech practice

Arlo is a proper noun; bag is a common noun. Carries and shine are verbs. Heavy and quiet are adjectives in these sentences. Slowly is an adverb describing carries. The word's sentence job supports the choice.

## word context challenge

Light describes box in the first sentence, so it is an adjective. A light names a thing in the second, so it is a noun. We light uses light as an action verb. Context supplies the grammatical job.

## capitalization practice

In April, I visit Sam in Boston. In begins the sentence; April, Sam and Boston are proper names; I is capitalized. Summer is a common season name, while Tuesday is a proper day name.

## name capitalization challenge

My aunt met Aunt Rosa at Pine Street Library. The first aunt is a common relationship label; Aunt Rosa is a name form. Pine Street Library names a particular library. An example comparison is We walked past a library to Pine Street Library.

## punctuation practice

We need paper, glue, and crayons. After breakfast, we draw. "I like the zebra," said Kit. "Where is the gate?" asked Kit. List commas separate items; the opening phrase takes a comma; the dialogue comma separates speech from its tag.

## dialogue list challenge

"Please bring the red, blue, and green pencils," said Liv. A question version is "Will you bring the red, blue, and green pencils?" asked Liv. The list commas remain; the final speech comma becomes a question mark, and asked matches the new speech.

## opinion paragraph practice

One model favors a reading corner because it offers a quiet place to focus and keeps books within reach. Pictures can show a seated reader and a low shelf. The reasons address quiet and access, not two repetitions of the word favorite.

## opinion reasons challenge

A model can explain that a large table gives room to spread paper and a shared supply tray keeps colors easy to find. First and also connect the reasons. The pictures need to show space and access to supplies, not merely a smiling person.

## opinion mini book practice

Possible reason pages show observing new leaves and working together to water. One line can change from The garden is nice to Measuring a new leaf helps us notice growth. The picture can show the leaf beside a ruler.

## opinion book order challenge

Place the cover first, followed by the opinion, the two reason pages and the closing. Add a short written reason to the page that relies entirely on the picture. The picture can enrich the argument while the text makes the main point accessible.

## story seed practice

Nico is in a park and wants visitors to read a concert poster. Wind folds it; holding one corner fails. Nico and Ren secure both corners to a fence. A new seed might follow a child trying to carry a fragile model through a narrow door.

## connected story challenge

One connected model has a fox waking to guide a supply boat through fog. The fox cannot see the dock marker and rings a dock bell to guide the boat safely. The same images now serve a goal, a complication and a resolution.

## agreement practice

The fox runs; the foxes run; I draw; the bag of shells is full. Subjects are fox, foxes, I and bag. Shells belongs to the modifying phrase of shells; bag controls the singular is.

## agreement challenge

The children are ready. Mina and Sol have a map. I walk slowly. Children is plural; the combined Mina and Sol subject is plural; I uses walk. A contrast is The kite rises and The kites rise.

## word choice practice

Animal or creature suits a calm neutral description; beast needs an intentionally different tone. Reason or explanation suits an accurate account, while excuse may suggest evasion. Nosy matches the privacy-crossing action most directly. A justified alternative must explain its changed effect.

## word tone challenge

Calm: The cat padded toward its cushion. Tense: The stray cat lunged toward the rattling gate. Padded and lunged create different movement and mood. Precise nouns clarify the scene without requiring unnecessary adjectives.

## narrative plan practice

One model begins with Ada delivering a tall paper tower. Tilting it bends the top. Then Ada notices its removable roof and says, "I can carry these separately." Both pieces pass safely and are reassembled. The events support examining a problem and adapting the plan.

## story causality challenge

A model adds a rattling sound when the character moves a cushion. The character checks the cushion seam and finds the key, then opens the locked box. The clue prompts an action, and the result follows from it instead of appearing by coincidence.

## picture spread practice

The left page can show the first boat tipping; the right can show the maker widening its base. A revision from The boats tips to The boat tips matches a singular subject. The pictures show the design difference while the text connects the attempt and revision.

## spread progression challenge

The first page can establish that a tall model does not fit. The second can show the character lifting off a removable top, with text explaining the new plan. The picture shows the changed shape; the words connect the choice to the obstacle.

## reading rehearsal practice

One model notices that the first attempt rushed the final spoken line. A second attempt pauses before the quotation and shows the floating-boat picture with the result. The observation concerns a specific feature rather than an unexplained rating.

## presentation picture challenge

Show the picture as the related event is read, then pause briefly to let the audience inspect it. For the poster story, the flapping-corner picture belongs with the difficulty and the open-poster picture with the solution.

## book completion practice

Complete the ordered page text, create pictures that support each page, revise the argument or event sequence and edit mechanics. Prepare the final pages, rehearse a reading and record one revision. A new reader should be able to follow the opinion or story using the book itself.

## book revision challenge

Revise an earlier attempt or clue so a character's action can lead to the resolution. Update the related middle scene and ending picture as well as the final sentence. Surface correctness and artwork support a story whose cause and outcome must also connect.
