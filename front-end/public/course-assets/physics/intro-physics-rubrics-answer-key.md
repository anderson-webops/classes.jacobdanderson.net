# Intro to Physics Rubrics and Answer Key

This guide supports feedback and self-checking for the supplied Intro to Physics materials. Accept equivalent reasoning when units, signs, evidence, and model assumptions are explicit. Do not reward a correct number that conflicts with the learner's graph, diagram, system boundary, or explanation.

## Shared Scoring Language

Score each dimension from 0–3:

| Dimension             | 3 — Secure                                                                            | 2 — Developing                                    | 1 — Beginning                                        | 0 — Missing                      |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| system and quantities | system, interval, direction, variables, and units are explicit                        | one minor element is unclear                      | several quantities or the system are ambiguous       | no usable setup                  |
| representation        | graph, diagram, table, or model is accurate and labeled                               | central idea is accurate with a minor omission    | representation contains a consequential mismatch     | absent or unrelated              |
| calculation           | equation or relationship, substitution, units, result, and reasonableness check agree | one minor arithmetic or notation issue            | method is partly relevant but meaning or units break | absent or unrelated              |
| evidence claim        | bounded claim cites at least two exact evidence points and reasoning                  | claim and evidence connect with one gap           | claim is broader than evidence or mainly descriptive | unsupported claim                |
| model honesty         | assumption, uncertainty, limitation, or alternative is specific                       | limitation is present but generic                 | limitation is confused with error or omitted detail  | model presented as literal proof |
| revision or transfer  | substantive revision or changed-condition prediction follows evidence                 | revision changes meaning but explanation is brief | cosmetic revision or unsupported prediction          | no revision                      |

A 12–15 score across the most relevant five dimensions indicates core-route readiness. A 15–18 score across all six dimensions indicates a strong stretch-route response. The numbers guide feedback; they do not replace review of scientific meaning.

## Measurement and Uncertainty Key

### Walking data

- Group A start-to-finish speed: \((4.8-0.0)/(4.0-0.0)=1.20\text{ m/s}\).
- Group B start-to-finish speed: \((4.7-0.1)/(4.0-0.0)=1.15\text{ m/s}\).
- Both series are consistent with nearly steady motion and small reading differences. The 0.05 m/s difference does not by itself establish that one method is wrong.
- A defensible report preserves the stated 0.10 m position and 0.05 s time resolutions. Reporting many extra digits would overstate the evidence.
- Random scatter can come from frame selection or reading a marker. A systematic bias can come from a shifted origin, scale calibration, or consistently early timestamp.

### Reaction-distance model

Using \(t=\sqrt{2d/g}\), the modeled times are approximately 0.157, 0.192, 0.175, 0.207, and 0.169 s. Mean ≈ 0.180 s; range ≈ 0.050 s.

The model assumes free fall, a correct distance reading, and an event marker that matches the start of the fall. The small sample describes these trials, not a person's permanent reaction ability.

### Quantity cards

The timestamp and image position are direct readings from the supplied record. Graph slope is calculated. Acceleration inferred from a trend is model-dependent and calculated. Reference \(g\) is a sourced model value. Distance from a not-to-scale illustration is an estimate without a defensible measurement scale.

Core evidence:

- correct unit conversions;
- labeled axes;
- a calculation trail;
- precision matched to source resolution;
- one random and one systematic uncertainty.

## Waves Sound and Light Key

All four wave-table cases have speed \(v=f\lambda=6.0\text{ m/s}\). A and B show doubled frequency with halved wavelength at the same modeled speed. B and C have the same frequency and wavelength but different amplitude.

The waveform has:

- amplitude 2.0 cm;
- period 1.0 s;
- frequency 1.0 Hz.

It is displacement versus time at one represented location, so wavelength is not directly readable without spatial information.

The resonance table peaks near 2.0 Hz. Increased damping commonly lowers and broadens the response peak in the introductory model.

For reflection, incidence and reflection angles are measured from the normal. Refraction direction depends on relative wave speed or refractive index. A path diagram alone does not determine reflected or transmitted energy fractions.

Core evidence:

- spatial and time graphs are identified;
- amplitude and frequency are never substituted for each other;
- sound and light medium requirements remain distinct;
- audio work has a waveform or table route.

## Core Capstone Key

Accept any one supplied investigation when it includes:

1. a narrow relationship question;
2. independent and dependent quantities;
3. controlled or fixed conditions;
4. preserved source values;
5. a fitting graph or representation;
6. one calculation with units;
7. a bounded CER response;
8. uncertainty or limitation;
9. an alternative or changed condition;
10. substantive revision.

Useful conclusions:

- ramp speed is not linear in height; speed is more directly proportional to \(\sqrt{h}\) in the ideal energy model;
- at fixed voltage, current decreases as resistance increases;
- the small-angle pendulum model is close to the supplied periods, with small differences that can reflect rounding, measurement, or nonideal behavior.

Weak-proposal corrections narrow the scope, define variables and graph choice in advance, preserve unexpected results, and avoid claiming that one matching prediction uniquely proves a model.

During the 0 °C plateau, energy transfer can continue while the represented material changes phase rather than increasing temperature.

Core evidence:

## Electromagnetism Key

The supplied moving-magnet and coil-turn tables are approximately proportional over the modeled range. Zero relative change gives no sustained induced signal in the record. Reversing motion reverses the sign or polarity under the same orientation convention.

Doubling coil turns doubles the supplied peak voltage. Real coils can deviate because of resistance, geometry, field nonuniformity, speed variation, heating, and measurement limits.

Motor and generator explanations run energy conversion in opposite directions:

- motor: electrical to mechanical, with losses;
- generator: mechanical to electrical, with losses.

Core evidence:

- presence of a field is distinguished from change in flux;
- direction conventions appear in diagrams;
- device vocabulary is connected to an input-output energy path;
- fields are represented, not described as visible material strings.

## Oscillation and Resonance Key

For 1.50 kg and \(k=10\text{ N/m}\):

\[
T=2\pi\sqrt{1.50/10}\approx2.43\text{ s}.
\]

The period follows \(\sqrt{m}\), not mass directly. The driven-response table peaks near 1.0 Hz. Higher damping lowers the peak and makes the response less sharply concentrated around the resonant region.

One full cycle returns to the same position and motion direction. Two equilibrium crossings can represent half or a full cycle depending on direction, so direction must accompany position.

Core evidence:

- equilibrium, amplitude, period, and frequency remain distinct;
- natural and driving frequencies are identified separately;
- damping and resonance are supported by graph evidence;
- simple harmonic motion is presented as an approximation.

## Modern Physics Key

The supplied threshold is near \(5.0\times10^{14}\text{ Hz}\). Above threshold, stopping potential increases with frequency in the record. At fixed 6.0×10¹⁴ Hz, increased intensity changes modeled electron count without materially changing the stopping potential.

The Unknown emission spectrum matches Source A across the four listed lines. This supports the match within the small supplied library; it does not rule out mixtures, calibration shifts, or sources absent from the table.

A strong model-limit response contains:

1. classical expectation;
2. observed evidence;
3. specific mismatch;
4. refined model feature;
5. application or prediction;
6. domain where the classical model remains useful;
7. remaining limitation.

Core evidence avoids treating quantum ideas as magic or claiming that one introductory dataset constitutes a complete theory.

## Final Portfolio Key

### Required evidence

Every portfolio contains:

- focused question or design problem;
- system, interval, and model;
- source record and raw supplied data;
- labeled representation;
- calculation with units and reasonableness check;
- criteria and constraints for design work;
- at least two options, conditions, or models;
- bounded claim or recommendation;
- uncertainty, limitation, and alternative;
- first version, feedback, substantive revision, and defense.

### Twenty-four-point rubric

Use all six shared dimensions for a maximum of 18 points, then add:

| Dimension                 | 3                                                               | 2                                   | 1                                     | 0                        |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| criteria and tradeoffs    | criteria, constraints, weighting, and tradeoff are explicit     | most decision factors are visible   | choice is made with little comparison | no defensible comparison |
| defense and accessibility | answers reversal question and provides equivalent access routes | defense or access route has one gap | defense mainly repeats the claim      | absent                   |

Interpretation:

- 20–24: secure synthesis with traceable revision;
- 15–19: complete core portfolio with targeted revisions remaining;
- 9–14: partial evidence chain requiring structural revision;
- 0–8: evidence chain not yet reviewable.

### Reversal question

The defense ends with: “What new evidence, operating condition, uncertainty result, or failed criterion would make you change this conclusion?” A valid answer names a concrete threshold or result rather than saying “more data.”
