# Algebra 1 Error Analysis

## Delivery Grid Distances

Grid distance adds the magnitudes of the horizontal and vertical displacements. For A it is abs(-3)+abs(5)=3+5=8 blocks; for B it is abs(4)+abs(6)=4+6=10 blocks. A is closer by 2 blocks. Taking abs(-3+5) allows movements on perpendicular streets to cancel and does not count the route length.

The direct A-to-B line has slope (6-5)/(4-(-3))=1/7. This ratio measures vertical coordinate change per horizontal coordinate change; it is not either trip's distance from the origin.

## Balancing Both Sides

The first invalid equality is 3x=19 after subtracting 4 only on the left. Subtract 4 on both sides to obtain 3x=15, then divide both sides by 3 to obtain x=5. Checking gives 3(5)+4=19.

The proposed 19/3 instead gives 3(19/3)+4=23, which fails the original right side of 19. Isolating a variable is useful only when the intervening equalities preserve the original equation.

## Distributing Whole Products

The first expansion omitted the products 7×(-8) and 3×(-4). The correct equation is 7x-56=3x-12. Subtracting 3x and adding 56 gives 4x=44, so x=11.

At 11, the original sides are 7(3)=21 and 3(7)=21. At the flawed candidate 1, they are 7(-7)=-49 and 3(-3)=-9. Later balancing steps can preserve the wrongly expanded equation without preserving the original one.

## Budget and Booth Hours

The two prices require 4x+5y=1000. With x=100, 400+5y=1000 gives y=120. The repaired mix costs 400+600=1000 dollars and contains 220 pieces. The flawed mix costs 4(100)+5(150)=1150 dollars, exceeding the budget by 150 dollars.

For a whole-hour booking h, capacity must satisfy 6h>=220. Thus h>=110/3, and the smallest integer booking is 37 hours. Thirty-six hours have capacity 216, while 37 have capacity 222; the last booking has room to finish the actual 220 pieces. Capacity is distinguished from the number of pieces actually produced or distributed.

## Comparing Community Trends

Library A's average rate is (12-6)/(4-1)=6/3=2 visits per month. The reversed quotient has units months per visit. Library B's rate is (10-7)/(4-1)=1 visit per month, so A grows faster in the supplied records.

The rules A(m)=2m+4 and B(m)=m+6 match all four supplied pairs. For example, A(1)=6, A(4)=12, B(1)=7 and B(4)=10. Extending those rules gives A(5)=14 and B(5)=11. These projections assume continuation of the modeled trends; fictional observations do not establish an effect of a real intervention.

## Scaling Entire Equations

Multiplication applies to both sides of each equation. The scaled equations are 16x+12y=32 and 15x-12y=30. Adding gives 31x=62, so x=2. Substituting into 4x+3y=8 gives y=0.

The checks are 4(2)+3(0)=8 and 5(2)-4(0)=10. The flawed cancellation is algebraically consistent with its two altered equations, but those altered equations no longer preserve the original constants.

## Recovering Intersection Coordinates

The factorization and roots are correct. The error begins when the first root is copied into the output coordinate. At x=-2, both rules give y=4: (-2)^2=4 and -(-2)+2=4. At x=1, both give y=1.

The intersections are (-2,4) and (1,1). For comparison, at x=-3 the outputs are 9 and 5, while at x=0 they are 0 and 2. Both actual intersections satisfy both equations. One correct pair does not remove the other valid root.

## Retaining Every Factored Root

Division by x+1 assumes x!=-1 and removes a possible solution. Apply the zero-product property instead: x-6=0 or x+1=0, giving x=6 or x=-1.

Checks in the original polynomial give 36-30-6=0 and 1+5-6=0. Both values belong to the stated real domain. A physical context could impose an additional restriction, but this problem supplies none excluding the negative root.

## Choosing the Variation Invariant

The ratio y/x is the direct-variation constant. In inverse variation the product is constant: k=xy=6(5)=30, so y=30/x with x!=0. At y=3, x=10. At x=12, y=2.5.

The products 6(5),10(3) and 12(2.5) all equal 30. The flawed pair instead gives (18/5)(3)=54/5=10.8. Doubling x from 6 to 12 halves y from 5 to 2.5, preserving the inverse relationship.

## Checking Both Inverse Directions

The untested composition gives g(f(-3))=sqrt(9)=3, which does not recover -3. On all real inputs, squaring maps both -3 and 3 to 9 and is not one-to-one.

Restrict f(x)=x^2 to x>=0. Its range is also [0,infinity). Then g(x)=sqrt(x) has the same domain and range. On these domains, g(f(x))=sqrt(x^2)=x because x>=0, and f(g(y))=(sqrt(y))^2=y because y>=0. For example, (3,9) on f reflects to (9,3) on g. The excluded pair (-3,9) explains why the restriction is necessary.
