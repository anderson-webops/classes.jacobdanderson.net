# Intro to Biology Rubrics and Answer Key


## Common Evidence and Model Rubric

Score each category as emerging, developing, secure, or transferable:

- **Evidence:** identifies a direct observation, datum, or source statement.
- **Scale:** distinguishes molecule, cell, tissue, organ, system, and organism claims.
- **Model:** shows relevant parts, interactions, boundary, and changed-condition prediction.
- **Reasoning:** explains why the evidence supports the claim without treating inference as observation.
- **Limitation:** names missing variables, representation limits, or uncertainty.
- **Accessibility and integrity:** provides text equivalents, uses color-independent labels, cites sources, and avoids personal health claims.

Transferable work can revise the model when evidence conflicts with the first explanation.

## Digestive Process Key

The gastrointestinal path runs mouth, esophagus, stomach, small intestine, large intestine, rectum, and anus. Liver, pancreas, and gallbladder are accessory organs in the introductory model. Food does not pass through them.

Mechanical actions change size, location, or mixing. Chemical digestion changes macromolecules through reactions supported by enzymes and conditions. Bile supports fat processing but is not an enzyme. Enzymes are catalysts, display substrate and condition dependence, and are not consumed as stoichiometric ingredients in the simplified model.

### BIO3 core checks

1. Mouth chewing/mixing and saliva precede coordinated pharyngeal swallowing and esophageal peristalsis. Food moves by coordinated muscular action, not gravity alone.
2. Ingestion is entry; mechanical digestion changes pieces/mixing; chemical digestion changes molecules; absorption crosses a lining; elimination removes the remaining tract mixture. Smaller pieces do not by themselves establish smaller molecules.
3. Epithelial cell, lining tissue, intestinal organ and digestive system are different scales. Bile and pancreatic digestive secretions reach the tube on separate arrows; food does not travel through those organs.
4. The highest tested E1 activity is 10 at pH 7. The substrate sequence is 0, 4, 7, 9, 9; the last two measurements form the supplied plateau. Neither table proves a universal optimum or maximum. E1/E2 specificity and condition dependence remain part of the explanation.

## Cellular Energy Key

Matter and energy are tracked separately. Carbon atoms from an organic molecule can appear in carbon dioxide or newly built molecules. Oxygen is a reactant in the overall aerobic-respiration model. Cellular respiration transfers part of the available chemical energy into processes that support ATP production, while energy also disperses as heat.

ATP is continuously produced and used as an energy-coupling molecule. It is not a calorie, a literal battery filled with energy, or the only molecule involved in cellular work. Personal calorie estimates are outside this evidence set.

3. The equation has 6 carbon, 12 hydrogen and 18 oxygen atoms on each side. It compresses many reactions; glycolysis begins in cytosol and later aerobic stages involve mitochondria. ATP/energy transfer is distinct from the matter outputs.
4. Organic molecules can originate through photosynthetic processes and later participate in respiration or biosynthesis. Plants also respire. The introductory chemical account does not convert disappearing carbon into an unlabelled energy output.

## Regulation Key

A negative-feedback model contains a changing condition, detection or signaling process, response, and reduction of the original deviation. Homeostasis is dynamic regulation around workable ranges, not a perfectly fixed state.

Signal arrows and material arrows are distinct. A graph can show timing or association without identifying a complete mechanism. An abstract supports only the population, comparison, and outcome it reports; it cannot automatically prove causation or predict an individual.

## Elimination and Excretion Key

- Undigested material and older intestinal-lining cells in stool connect to elimination.
- Carbon dioxide released through lungs and nitrogen-containing waste in urine connect to excretion.
- Water moving from the large intestine into the body is reabsorption.
- A nutrient entering blood is absorption and transport.
- Heat leaving the body is energy transfer rather than matter excretion.
- A label without pathway or boundary may require “not enough information.”

Kidneys filter blood and selectively reabsorb needed substances; urine is not simply “filtered food.” The liver processes many substances but is not the route by which every waste leaves the body.

## Capstone Defense Rubric

The defense addresses:

1. What is the system boundary and narrow claim?
2. Which evidence is direct, and which statement is inferred?
3. How does the explanation connect molecule, cell or tissue, organ, and system scales?
4. Where do matter, energy, and information move?
5. Which model feature is useful, and what does it hide?
6. What alternative explanation or missing variable matters?
7. What changed-condition result would weaken the claim?
8. How did feedback change the model or evidence chain?

A complete capstone is traceable, accessible, and bounded. It does not use personal medical data, make a diagnosis, prescribe nutrition, or claim mastery of biology topics not taught in the Digestive Odyssey.

### BIO8 core checks

1. A representation is chosen for its question: tube order, epithelial crossing or complete text route. It identifies preserved and omitted features and has a text equivalent.
2. The portfolio links molecule, cell/tissue, organ and system claims without claiming a complete genetics/evolution/ecology survey. Missing exact mechanisms remain labelled.
3. One required capstone may use the saved core notes or the supplied worked record. Its evidence log, route, mechanism, energy/storage model, feedback loop, waste distinction, changed-condition prediction and limitation are explicit. Preserve a first and revised biological model.
4. The defense cites two relevant source details and explains the mechanism, alternative and result that would change the claim. Correcting whole-meal → liver into a tube plus absorbed-product blood route is a substantive revision.

### Optional checks

- The exhibit continues the saved required artifact with one additional condition or representation.
- The CER presentation reuses one narrow claim and relevant evidence from that artifact.
- The reflection cites two concrete revisions and a next question. These optional products do not require another complete capstone.
