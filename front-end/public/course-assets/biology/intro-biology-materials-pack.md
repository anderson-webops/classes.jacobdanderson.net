# Intro to Biology Materials Pack

Use these supplied cases, tables, and evidence records instead of requiring food handling, body measurements, medical disclosure, or household experiments. Every model labels its system boundary, distinguishes observation from inference, and remains usable without color.

## Course Evidence and Accessibility Routine

For each investigation, record:

1. the biological question and the system boundary;
2. the direct observation, supplied datum, or source statement;
3. the model used to explain the evidence;
4. the scale involved: molecule, organelle, cell, tissue, organ, organ system, or organism;
5. one inference and one limitation;
6. a changed-condition prediction;
7. a text description for every diagram, graph, or image.

Personal diet, symptoms, diagnoses, weight, body measurements, and medical history are never required evidence. Fictional cases and supplied values are the default.

## Body Systems Scenario Cards

### Scenario A: Exercise and Heat

A fictional person begins a steady walk in warm conditions. Breathing rate and heart rate rise, skin blood flow increases, and sweating begins. The evidence does not identify the exact signal timing or quantify hydration.

Map respiratory gas exchange, circulatory transport, muscular demand, nervous or endocrine information, and integumentary heat transfer. Mark material-flow arrows separately from information-flow arrows.

### Scenario B: Food-Allergy Model

A supplied diagram shows an immune response associated with a food protein. Treat the diagram as a simplified system model, not a diagnosis or a prediction about any individual. Identify the digestive boundary, immune interaction, circulatory connection, and one missing variable.

### Scenario C: Calcium Pathway

A meal model contains a calcium source. Trace calcium from the digestive lumen across an intestinal boundary and into circulation, then name one possible use in body structures or signaling. The scenario supports a pathway model, not a recommendation about intake.

## Absorption and Membrane Transport Cases

### Case A: Concentration Difference

Side 1 contains 18 model particles and Side 2 contains 4. A membrane permits those particles to cross without a transport protein. Predict net movement, then explain why particles still move in both directions.

### Case B: Protein-Assisted Movement

The membrane blocks a supplied solute unless a matching channel or carrier is present. Compare simple diffusion with facilitated diffusion without claiming that either process requires the cell to “want” equilibrium.

### Case C: Movement Against a Gradient

The model moves a solute toward the side with greater concentration and labels ATP use. Classify the process as active transport and identify the energy-coupling evidence.

### Case D: Intestinal Route Choice

The supplied pathway sends simple sugars and amino acids into blood vessels and sends many absorbed lipid products first through lymphatic vessels. Treat the routes as an introductory model; name which tissue boundary is crossed and what the diagram leaves out.

## Cellular Energy Ledger

Use this atom-and-energy ledger rather than a personal calorie estimate:

| Stage                      | Carbon-containing input  | Oxygen role                           | Carbon-containing output     | Energy account                                                          |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Digestion                  | macromolecule model      | no direct role assigned here          | smaller molecule units       | chemical bonds are rearranged through reactions                         |
| Absorption and transport   | small nutrient molecules | transported separately                | molecules delivered to cells | transport can require cellular work                                     |
| Cellular respiration model | glucose model            | reactant in the overall aerobic model | carbon dioxide               | some transferred energy supports ATP production; some disperses as heat |
| Biosynthesis or storage    | small molecules          | depends on pathway                    | larger biological molecules  | building molecules requires coupled reactions                           |

ATP is a short-term energy-transfer molecule, not energy itself and not a long-term storage tank. The overall cellular-respiration equation is an accounting model; it does not show every intermediate reaction or imply that energy is created.

## Feedback and Regulation Cases

### Case A: Post-Meal Glucose Model

A supplied graph rises after a fictional meal and returns toward a reference range. Build a negative-feedback model with stimulus, sensor or signaling source, signal, target response, and reduced deviation. The graph does not diagnose a person or prove the action of one hormone by itself.

### Case B: Digestive Secretion

A stretch or chemical cue is followed by a signal and a change in digestive secretion. Separate the cue, information pathway, responding tissue, released material, and measured outcome.

### Case C: Abstract Evidence

An abstract reports an association between a microbial pattern and one measured digestive outcome. Record population, comparison, result, and limitation. Association is not automatically causation, a group average is not an individual prediction, and one microbial taxon is not the entire microbiome.

## Elimination and Excretion Sort

Classify each material and justify the system boundary:

- undigested material remaining in the digestive tract;
- water reabsorbed from the large intestine;
- carbon dioxide produced by cellular respiration and released through the lungs;
- nitrogen-containing waste carried in blood and processed into urine;
- excess water and dissolved substances leaving through urine;
- older intestinal-lining cells present in stool;
- heat transferred to the surroundings;
- a nutrient absorbed into blood.

Use the categories elimination, excretion, transport, reabsorption, or not enough information. Feces and urine are not interchangeable categories, and “waste” does not mean biologically identical material.

## Digestive Odyssey Capstone Packet

Submit:

1. a narrow question about one fictional meal component;
2. a source log with organization, title, link, evidence role, and limitation;
3. a molecule-to-cell-to-system pathway;
4. one digestive or transport mechanism;
5. one cellular-use or storage model;
6. one regulation loop;
7. an elimination or excretion distinction;
8. a changed-condition prediction;
9. one model revision with before-and-after evidence;
10. a statement of what the project cannot establish.

The final representation includes text equivalents for visual evidence and preserves uncertainty. It explains a human-biology case study; it does not provide dietary advice, diagnose health, or substitute for the broader study of genetics, evolution, ecology, and biological diversity.
