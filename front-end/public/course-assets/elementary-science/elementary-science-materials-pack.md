# Elementary Science Materials Pack

These supplied phenomena, descriptions, and fictional instructional datasets make every core task possible with a shared screen and paper or digital notes. No activity requires household materials, outdoor access, a camera, hearing or vision screening, personal information, or a physical experiment.

## Science Notebook and Response Frames

Record the source title and date before responding.

### K–2 response choices

- **I observe:** I can point to, draw, select, dictate, or write ___.
- **I think:** One possible explanation is ___.
- **My evidence:** I noticed ___ in the source.
- **I wonder:** What would happen if ___?
- **My model shows:** ___.
- **I would improve:** ___.

### Grades 3–5 response fields

1. phenomenon and question;
2. direct observations or supplied data;
3. table, graph, sort, or labeled model;
4. claim;
5. evidence with exact source detail;
6. reasoning using target vocabulary;
7. alternate explanation or model limitation;
8. changed-condition prediction;
9. revision after feedback or test.

Speaking, pointing, selecting, dictating, drawing, typing, and handwriting can demonstrate the same science understanding. A visual response includes a text description and never relies on color alone.

## Observation Inference and Fair Comparison Cards

### Mystery scene A: schoolyard after rain

| Source detail | Direct observation                         |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| ground image  | three puddles appear beside a curb         |
| leaf image    | several leaves have droplets               |
| sky image     | clouds cover most of the pictured sky      |
| ruler overlay | the deepest pictured puddle is marked 3 cm |
| time card     | the scene is labeled 9:00 a.m.             |

Possible inferences include recent rain, sprinkler use, uneven ground, or blocked drainage. The scene alone does not identify which explanation is correct.

### Mystery scene B: two plant models

| Case | Light card | Water card  | Day 1 height | Day 8 height | Leaf note        |
| ---- | ---------- | ----------- | -----------: | -----------: | ---------------- |
| P    | 8 hours    | 20 mL daily |         5 cm |         9 cm | six green leaves |
| Q    | 2 hours    | 20 mL daily |         5 cm |         6 cm | four pale leaves |

These are fictional instructional values. Grades 3–5 can identify light as the changed condition in this simplified comparison, but one pair of model plants and one week cannot establish a universal plant-growth rule.

### Statement sort

- “The graph line rises from 4 to 9.” — observation from supplied data.
- “More light caused every plant to grow.” — overgeneralized causal claim.
- “Case P grew more in this table.” — bounded comparison.
- “The plant liked the sunny place.” — personified inference.
- “Would the pattern repeat with more cases?” — testable follow-up question.

| Case               | Supplied evidence                                                                 | Classification boundary   |

### Structure-function comparison

| Organism | Structure   | Visible feature                | Possible function in the supplied habitat                       | Limitation                                         |
| -------- | ----------- | ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| duck     | webbed feet | skin connects toes             | pushes against water while swimming                             | not evidence that every water bird has webbed feet |
| cactus   | thick stem  | stores visible volume          | stores water and supports photosynthesis in many cactus species | species and habitat conditions differ              |
| fox      | dense fur   | layered covering               | helps reduce heat loss in cold conditions                       | may be costly in hot conditions                    |
| mangrove | prop roots  | branching roots above wet soil | support and gas exchange in waterlogged settings                | not the only mangrove adaptation                   |

Learners may invent a creature, but invented features remain design choices. They do not become evidence that a real organism exists or that evolution works toward a planned goal.

## Food Web and Population Cases

### Fictional pond web

- Sunlight → algae and pond plants.
- Algae → water flea → minnow → heron.
- Pond plants → snail → sunfish → heron.
- Dead material and waste → decomposers.
- Decomposer activity returns matter to water and sediment reservoirs.

The arrow convention is food or energy source → receiver. Use labels or line patterns in addition to arrows and color.

### Population table

| Period | Algae index | Water-flea count | Minnow count | Heron observations | Observation hours |
| ------ | ----------: | ---------------: | -----------: | -----------------: | ----------------: |
| A      |          40 |               30 |           18 |                  4 |                 8 |
| B      |          55 |               42 |           20 |                  5 |                 8 |
| C      |          75 |               28 |           13 |                  3 |                 4 |
| D      |          62 |               24 |           10 |                  3 |                 8 |

Raw heron observations and observation rate answer different questions. The table can support descriptions and competing hypotheses, not a single proven cause for every change.

### Habitat-change cards

1. Shade increases over part of the pond.
2. Runoff adds nutrients after a storm.
3. A dry period lowers water depth.
4. Observation effort is cut in half.

For each card, distinguish the supplied change, a predicted direct effect, a possible indirect effect, and the evidence needed to check the prediction.

## Forces Motion and Machines Data

### Cart story

| Time | Position from start | Supplied force state                       |
| ---- | ------------------: | ------------------------------------------ |
| 0 s  |                 0 m | cart at rest                               |
| 1 s  |                 1 m | forward push larger than opposing friction |
| 2 s  |                 3 m | forward push continues                     |
| 3 s  |                 5 m | simplified balanced horizontal-force case  |
| 4 s  |                 7 m | simplified balanced horizontal-force case  |

In the simplified final intervals, unchanged speed can coexist with balanced horizontal forces. A flat position-time graph would indicate unchanged position, not automatically no forces.

### Force-arrow cards

1. book resting on a table: upward support and downward gravity;
2. cart speeding right: rightward applied force larger than leftward resistance;
3. tug-of-war marker staying at one position: equal opposite horizontal pulls in the supplied case;
4. rolling ball slowing: resistance opposes the motion after the push ends.

### Simple-machine comparison

| Method        | Supplied force rating | Distance moved by effort | Setup note           |
| ------------- | --------------------: | -----------------------: | -------------------- |
| lift directly |                     5 |                   1 unit | shortest path        |
| long ramp     |                     2 |                  4 units | needs more space     |
| short ramp    |                     3 |                  2 units | steeper              |
| lever         |                     2 |                  3 units | needs a stable pivot |

These qualitative ratings demonstrate a tradeoff. They are not measurements of total work or efficiency.

1. lamp → book → reflected light → eye;
2. Sun → opaque tree → blocked path → shadow region on ground;
3. lamp → clear sheet → transmitted light → screen;
4. lamp → dark cloth → mostly absorbed light in the simplified case.

Every diagram includes a source, interaction, path, and receiver. Eyes receive light; they do not send seeing rays.

## Sky Pattern and Planet Model Cards

### Day-night sequence

Four cards show one location at sunrise, midday, sunset, and night. The Earth-rotation model uses a lamp symbol for the Sun and a marked point on a globe diagram. The model represents changing illumination as Earth rotates; it omits scale, orbital motion during one day, atmosphere, and the Sun's enormous size and distance.

| ------- | -------------------------: | ----------------------: | ----------------------------: | --------------------------------------------- |
| Earth   |                       1.00 |                    1.00 |                          1.00 | rocky surface, nitrogen-oxygen atmosphere     |
| Mars    |                       0.53 |                    1.52 |                          1.88 | rocky surface, thin carbon-dioxide atmosphere |
| Jupiter |                       11.2 |                    5.20 |                         11.86 | gas giant with no solid outer surface         |

Use NASA Space Place for age-appropriate source reading before replacing rounded values. A card may preserve property comparisons but cannot preserve both planetary sizes and interplanetary distances on one ordinary page.

### Model critique cards

- Model A preserves planet order but exaggerates planet size.
- Model B compares relative diameter but omits distance.
- Model C shows one orbit pattern but not real speed or inclination.
- Model D shows surface details but not interior or atmosphere depth.

## Elementary Science Capstone Packet

Choose one supplied phenomenon from this pack or one teacher-approved source.

1. State the question or simple design problem.
2. Name the K–2 or grades 3–5 response route.
3. List target vocabulary.
4. Identify the exact evidence source.
5. Build a labeled model, table, graph, sort, or design.
6. Explain the claim with evidence and reasoning.
7. If designing, name criteria, constraints, and at least two possible ideas.
8. Compare or test one changed condition.
9. Record one failure, limitation, or alternate explanation.
10. Revise and preserve the before-and-after evidence.
11. Add a text description for visual work and a visual or text route for audio.
12. Answer: What evidence would change this explanation or design?

No physical build is required. A drawing, storyboard, simulation state, or paper/browser model can provide the plan-test-improve evidence.
