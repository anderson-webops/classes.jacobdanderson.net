# Elementary Science Rubrics and Answer Key

## Common Two Band Evidence Rubric

Assess each category as starting, growing, secure, or extending:

- **Phenomenon and source:** identifies what is being studied and where evidence comes from.
- **Observation:** points to, draws, selects, dictates, or writes a direct source detail.
- **Representation:** uses a labeled drawing, sort, table, graph, or model that another person can follow.
- **Explanation:** connects evidence to a claim with age-appropriate reasoning.
- **Boundary:** distinguishes observation, inference, model, prediction, and preference.
- **Revision:** changes an idea or design after comparison, feedback, or test evidence.
- **Accessibility:** provides text equivalents and does not rely on color, audio, handwriting, or physical access alone.

For K–2, secure work can be oral, selected, dictated, or drawn and includes a source detail plus one because connection. For grades 3–5, secure work adds organized data or model evidence, a fuller reasoning chain, and one limitation or changed-condition prediction.

## Science Practices Key

An observation reports what the supplied source shows. An inference is a possible explanation. A claim answers the question. Evidence is a relevant source detail. Reasoning explains why that detail supports the claim.

In Mystery Scene A, puddles, droplets, cloud cover, depth, and time are observations. Rain, a sprinkler, blocked drainage, or uneven ground are possible explanations. The image cannot choose among them without more evidence.

In Mystery Scene B, P grows 4 cm and Q grows 1 cm. The simplified comparison changes the light card while holding the supplied water amount equal. It supports “P grew more in this table,” not “more light always makes every plant grow.”

### ES1 core checks

1. Three puddles, leaf droplets and cloud cover are direct source details. Recent rain and a sprinkler are possible explanations. In the statement sort: a graph rising from 4 to 9 is an observation; “more light caused every plant” exceeds the evidence; P growing more is a bounded comparison; “liked” is a personified inference; repetition is a follow-up question.
2. P: 9 − 5 = 4 cm; Q: 6 − 5 = 1 cm. The supplied light duration changes and water amount stays equal. More cases and information about other conditions would strengthen the comparison.
3. Puddles and droplets support a claim about wet surfaces. They alone cannot select rain over a sprinkler. Accept a different cause only when it fits the actual details and is labelled as an inference.
4. Growth bars end at 4 and 1 cm with equally spaced units and a zero baseline. Final-height bars at 9 and 6 cm would answer a question about final height rather than amount grown.
5. A useful revision changes “rain made these puddles” to “rain or a sprinkler could explain the wet surfaces.” Retain the same observations and identify what additional evidence could distinguish the causes.
6. “Does the growth pattern repeat with additional comparable plants?” is testable. A corrected conclusion applies to the supplied P/Q cases and names the limits of the small model comparison.

## Living Things Key

Movement, spreading, using fuel, or becoming larger is not enough to classify something as living. Use multiple life-process criteria and preserve once-living and dormant categories.

Plants use light energy, water, carbon dioxide, and materials from their environment to make and build organic matter. Soil supplies water and minerals but is not food in the everyday sense. Structure-function claims are conditional: webbed feet can support swimming, but one trait does not guarantee survival or prove every habitat relationship.

### ES2 core checks

1. Bean plant and dormant seed are living; robot and rock are nonliving. Wood is once-living material. A flame's fuel use and spread do not supply cellular organization or biological reproduction.
2. Plant and snail both need water and suitable conditions. Plants make sugars using light, carbon dioxide and water; snails obtain food by eating. Soil supplies water and minerals rather than acting as food eaten by the plant.
3. Webbed feet are a structure, pushing water is a supported function, and paddling is behavior. Dense fur can reduce heat loss in cold conditions but make heat loss harder in warm conditions.
4. Stored water can buffer a cactus against dry conditions, within limits. Mangrove roots support functions in waterlogged settings. Neither feature guarantees survival in the other's environment or removes every environmental need.
5. The resting seed retains living organization and can resume growth under suitable conditions. The robot is a designed system. Crystal growth adds material without biological life processes. Use several criteria rather than motion alone.
6. Accept an invented feature when its habitat, proposed job and limitation are explicit. Label it as a model. Real organisms do not choose needed new inherited body parts; a plausible advantage still needs evidence about the organism and environment.

## Ecosystems Key

The course arrow convention is source of food or energy → receiver. Energy enters most modeled food webs through sunlight and producers, moves through consumers, and disperses as heat. Decomposers transform dead material and waste and return matter to environmental reservoirs; they do not recycle energy.

Heron observation rates are 0.5, 0.625, 0.75, and 0.375 observations per hour. Period C has fewer raw observations but fewer observation hours. Population, encounter rate, and cause remain different claims.

### ES3 core checks

1. Plants, snails and fish are living; water, sunlight and temperature are nonliving components. The ecosystem includes their interactions; a habitat names a place and its conditions for an organism.
2. Both supplied chains lead from producer to consumer and eventually to heron. The legend distinguishes energy-transfer arrows from matter-return routes. A hypothetical added food link remains labelled as a prediction.
3. Pond plants are producers, snails consumers, and the supplied decomposer group processes dead material and waste. Material from every trophic level can enter that route. Energy disperses as heat rather than returning as recycled food energy.
4. Minnow values are 18, 20, 13 and 10. The maximum is B, 20; the A-to-D change is 10 − 18 = −8. The values alone do not identify the cause or demonstrate every organism's abundance.
5. Extra shade could reduce plant growth, which could affect snails directly through food and sunfish or herons indirectly. Accept other coherent routes supported by the supplied web. Keep these as predictions and name a measurement that could test them.
6. A: 4/8 = 0.5; B: 5/8 = 0.625; C: 3/4 = 0.75; D: 3/8 = 0.375 observations per hour. C has a higher observed rate than A despite its smaller raw count. Neither rate measures the whole heron population.

## Weather Water and Earth Key

The seven-day table supports exact weather descriptions and a limited short forecast discussion. It cannot establish climate.

Water pools include ocean, atmosphere, soil, groundwater, ice, and organisms. Flows include evaporation, condensation-related state change, precipitation, runoff, infiltration, and transpiration. The Sun drives much evaporation; gravity drives falling and downhill movement.

Weathering breaks material down, erosion moves it, and deposition places it. A fast event can begin longer-lasting changes, so fast and slow are time-scale descriptions rather than mutually exclusive categories for an entire landscape.

### ES4 core checks

1. Day 4 is 6 °C warmer than Day 1 and has 0 rather than 6 mm of rain. Day 7 has the largest measured rain amount, 9 mm. Wind uses km/h; the temperature table records daily highs.
2. The fictional Day 8 forecast gives an 18 °C high, 60% rain chance, 18 km/h wind and mostly cloudy sky. Keep these predictions separate from the observed week. A rain-cover recommendation can follow the forecast while retaining its uncertainty.
3. A valid route includes a water store and labelled processes such as evaporation, condensation and precipitation. Evaporation is liquid to gas; condensation is gas to liquid. Accept either complete runoff or groundwater branch and a correctly labelled plant route.
4. The plotted daily highs are 18, 19, 22, 24, 23, 20 and 17 °C. Maximum: Day 4, 24 °C; minimum: Day 7, 17 °C. Equal vertical intervals represent equal temperature differences. A single week cannot establish climate.
5. The supplied flash flood is a fast event; soil formation spans many years. Weathering breaks material down, erosion transports it and deposition leaves it elsewhere. A flood can move sediment quickly and initiate effects that continue afterward.
6. Infiltration enters the ground; groundwater can feed a spring and river. Runoff instead travels over the surface. Sunlight supplies much evaporation energy and gravity moves falling/downhill water. The pumping branch does not supply withdrawal amounts or evidence of sustainability.

## Matter and Materials Key

Observable properties and particle models answer different questions. Gases are matter. Solid particles move in the model even when a solid keeps its shape. Dissolved salt remains in the system even when grains are no longer visible.

A material recommendation is conditional on criteria and constraints. Clear plastic supports transparency and water resistance; cloth supports absorbency and flexibility; other jobs can favor other options. Reversible classifications state what process and recovery boundary are used.

### ES5 core checks

1. Ice is solid before melting, flowing water is liquid, and air is gas. Pourable sand consists of solid grains. Air has mass and occupies space even when it is not visible.
2. Clear plastic scores 5 for both water resistance and transparency and 2 for mass. Cloth scores 1, 1 and 2; aluminum scores 5, 1 and 3. Only plastic meets the original cover requirements.
3. Cloth's absorbency is 5 and it meets the wipe requirement. Clear plastic and aluminum each score 1 for absorbency. The preferred choice changes with the job.
4. Adding flexibility ≥4 rejects plastic's rating 3. Rubber has flexibility and water resistance 5 but transparency 1 and mass 3. No listed material passes all four requirements. A justified revision changes the design or a requirement, or proposes a new material with a planned test; it does not invent data.
5. Cooling wax recovers solid wax but not necessarily the original shape. Salt can remain recoverable after dissolving if the water is separated. Rust is not readily returned to iron by the supplied simple process. A classification needs its recovery boundary.
6. The disappearance of visible salt grains does not mean the salt or its matter disappeared. Particle motion and spacing are model explanations. Stable solid shape is compatible with particle motion.

## Forces and Machines Key

Force arrows represent pushes or pulls on the selected object. Balanced forces mean zero net force in the simplified case, not necessarily zero motion. A position-time graph describes position over time; a flat section means unchanged position, while equal position changes over equal time indicate constant speed.

The machine table shows lower supplied force ratings paired with longer effort distances or setup needs. It supports a force-distance tradeoff, not the claim that machines create energy or remove all work.

### ES6 core checks

1. Gravity pulls the book down and the table supports it upward. The cart can receive a forward applied push and opposing resistance. Draw arrows on the receiving object and identify their source when stated.
2. The cart's interval changes are 1, 2, 2 and 2 m over successive one-second intervals. The final three average speeds are each 2 m/s in this supplied table.
3. The resting book can have balanced vertical forces; the final cart intervals have balanced horizontal forces and constant motion. If resistance exceeds the forward push, the net horizontal force opposes the cart's forward motion, changing its velocity.
4. Plot (0,0), (1,1), (2,3), (3,5) and (4,7), with time in seconds and position in metres. A separate horizontal segment describes unchanged position. Graph height is position; change in position over time describes average speed.
5. The cards identify ramp, lever, fixed pulley, wheel and axle, wedge and screw. The single fixed pulley changes pull direction without multiplying force. The lever needs its pivot; the ramp uses a sloping path.

## Light Sound and Signals Key

Light travels source → interaction → receiver. Objects are seen when light reaches an eye or camera after emission or reflection. A shadow is a lower-light region formed when an object blocks part of a path.

Sound begins with vibration, travels through matter in the course model, and is detected by a receiver. Pitch and loudness are different features. A reliable signal has a clear message, distinct pattern, intended receiver, and accessible alternative when noise, visibility, color, hearing, or ambiguity creates a barrier.

### ES7 core checks

1. The reflected-light path is lamp → book → eye. The four cards show reflection, blocking, transmission and absorption. The shadow falls on the ground surface; a transmitted-light screen receives light; the dark cloth absorbs much of the incident light in the model.
2. Sender: base; message: return to base; code: three beeps; medium: air; receiver: the supplied team receiver. Noise can prevent detection; the visible text provides a separate route to the same meaning.
3. Shadow A is 4 units high and B is 2. A's object is nearer the point source; source, screen and object size are held fixed. The repaired arrow direction reaches the eye from the lamp through reflection at the book. The model omits a real extended source's partly lit shadow edges.
4. B's 400 vibrations per second predict higher pitch than A's 200 at the same amplitude rating. A and C have the same frequency and predicted pitch; C has the higher amplitude rating and predicted greater loudness under the controlled conditions. A rating is not a decibel measurement.
5. The first design succeeds in quiet and fails in noise. Beeps plus text succeed in noise when the screen is visible; both fail when noise blocks sound and the screen is obscured. The revision improves the specified criterion without passing every condition.
6. Words, patterns or another distinguishable route can carry the message without color alone. Accept a proposed extra route with a relevant test. Preserve the obscured-screen failure and keep an untested proposal separate from recorded success.

## Sky and Models Key

Repeated sky observations establish patterns. The day-night model uses Earth rotation to explain changing illumination at one location. The Sun's apparent daily motion is not evidence that it travels around Earth each day.

Solar-system models trade accuracy dimensions. A model can preserve planet order, one relative size comparison, one orbit pattern, or selected surface details while omitting or distorting other features. Planet comparisons use the same property and unit rather than judging from drawing size.

### ES8 core checks

1. The model order is sunrise → midday → sunset → night. Both days repeat it as the marked point rotates into and out of illumination. Actual daylight length depends on location and season, which the simplified case omits.
2. An AU is approximately the average Earth–Sun distance. Mars is 1.52 − 1.00 = 0.52 AU farther from the Sun than Earth in the rounded comparison; its diameter is 0.53 of Earth's and its year 1.88 Earth years. The Moon's listed 0.075-year orbit is around Earth, not the Sun.
3. Rotation is turning about an axis; orbit is motion around another body. Moon phases show changing views of its sunlit half as it orbits Earth, over about 29.5 days. A lunar eclipse instead requires Earth's shadow to fall on the Moon in a particular alignment.
4. Model A supports order, and Model B supports relative size. Each omits or distorts other features. A page using a useful planet-size scale cannot ordinarily also show true interplanetary distances on that same scale.
5. Accept one required capstone that cites actual supplied evidence, explains the representation and preserves a revision. Grade-band responses differ in depth rather than requiring two artifacts. The signal worked record demonstrates question, evidence, reasoning, improvement and remaining limit.
6. In the worked record, text is the design change; correct receipt in quiet and visible-screen noise is the criterion; use of the supplied speaker/screen is the constraint. The obscured-screen failure remains a limit. Apply the same distinctions to the saved capstone. Its optional continuation adds a changed condition or new evidence to that artifact.

## Capstone Defense Rubric

The capstone answers:

1. What phenomenon, question, or design problem is being studied?
2. Which supplied evidence supports the response?
3. What does the representation show?
4. What is directly observed, inferred, modeled, or predicted?
5. How does the explanation differ for K–2 and grades 3–5?
6. If designing, what are the criteria, constraints, and alternatives?
7. What comparison or test was used?
8. What failed, remained uncertain, or lay outside the model?
9. What changed from the first version to the final version?
10. What evidence would change the explanation or design?
11. Can the work be understood through a text route without color, sound, physical materials, or personal disclosure?

A polished drawing or presentation cannot replace evidence and revision. K–2 work can satisfy the rubric through oral, selected, dictated, or drawn responses. Grades 3–5 work adds organized data, fuller CER reasoning, and a limitation or changed-condition prediction.
